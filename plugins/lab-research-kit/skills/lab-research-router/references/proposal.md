# Master's and doctoral proposal workflow

Use this reference for 开题报告, proposal defense, research plans, technical routes, feasibility analyses, and milestone schedules.

## 1. Intake and template handling

Inspect the institution's real template first when supplied. Preserve required headings, word limits, citation style, signatures, and review fields. If no template exists, use the generic structure below but label it as a proposal, not an approved institutional form.

Before drafting, establish:

- degree, discipline, working title, supervisor-approved direction, and deadline;
- research question, objectives, hypothesis or exploratory purpose;
- existing literature and pilot evidence;
- samples/data, facilities, techniques, collaborators, budget, and time;
- required ethics, animal, biosafety, clinical, data, or facility approvals;
- expected thesis contribution and minimum acceptable outcome.

## 2. Argument architecture

Build the proposal in this order:

1. **Problem** — define the phenomenon, population/system, context, and measurable consequence.
2. **Evidence** — summarize verified current knowledge and disagreement without turning summaries into proof.
3. **Gap** — state what is unknown, why existing designs cannot resolve it, and how the search was bounded.
4. **Question/hypothesis** — make it answerable and falsifiable, or explicitly exploratory.
5. **Objectives** — use one primary objective and limited secondary objectives.
6. **Methods** — map each objective to design, material/sample, measurement, comparison, endpoint, and analysis.
7. **Expected interpretation** — describe possible result patterns, not fabricated favorable results.
8. **Contribution** — separate theoretical, methodological, empirical, and practical contributions.
9. **Feasibility and risk** — document resources, approvals, dependencies, failure modes, and fallback routes.
10. **Schedule and outputs** — attach gates and artifacts to milestones, not only calendar dates.

Avoid claims such as “国内外尚无研究” unless a defensible systematic search supports them. Prefer “在已检索的数据库、时间范围和关键词下，未发现直接回答该问题的研究”。

## 3. Technical-route contract

For each objective, create a row:

| Objective | Material/sample | Intervention/factor | Comparator/control | Primary endpoint | Measurement | Analysis | Success/pivot rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Render the route only after this table is coherent. A diagram cannot repair a missing control or an endpoint that does not answer the objective.

Use this logical form:

```text
Research question
→ operational definitions
→ work package/objective
→ design and controls
→ measurement and quality control
→ analysis and uncertainty
→ interpretation boundary
→ decision / next experiment
```

## 4. Feasibility and risk

Evaluate at least:

- access to samples, data, equipment, reagents, compute, and trained operators;
- realistic throughput, procurement lead time, failure/repeat rate, and maintenance windows;
- sample-size or information sufficiency;
- recruitment, attrition, batch, contamination, assay, model, and integration risks;
- ethics, consent, animal welfare, biosafety, chemical safety, privacy, and intellectual-property constraints;
- whether the project still yields a defensible thesis if the main hypothesis is unsupported.

Create a risk register with likelihood, impact, early warning, mitigation, fallback, owner, and decision date.

## 5. Proposal defense

Prepare a 10–15 minute version unless the institution specifies otherwise:

1. one-sentence problem and importance;
2. closest known work and precise gap;
3. RQ, hypothesis, and objectives;
4. technical route and key controls;
5. novelty/contribution with evidence boundaries;
6. feasibility, preliminary basis, and resources;
7. schedule, risks, and fallback;
8. expected outputs and limitations.

Generate likely questions across novelty, mechanism, measurement validity, controls, sample size, statistics, feasibility, ethics/safety, failure routes, and thesis contribution. Draft answer cards with `claim → evidence → limitation → action`; never invent pilot results to strengthen an answer.

## 6. Completion gate

Do not call the proposal ready until:

- every objective maps to a method, endpoint, and analysis;
- critical citations and novelty claims have verification status;
- experimental units, controls, replication, and sample-size logic are defensible;
- approvals and high-risk dependencies are visible;
- schedule includes decision gates and fallback time;
- contribution remains meaningful under at least one plausible null or failed route;
- the researcher and supervisor make the final scope decision.
