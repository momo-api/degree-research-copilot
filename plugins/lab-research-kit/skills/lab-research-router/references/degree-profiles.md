# Degree and project profiles

Use this reference to prevent a one-size-fits-all workflow. Academic integrity does not change by degree; scope, originality expectations, artifact count, and methodological complexity do.

## 1. Degree profiles

| Profile | Default scope | Contribution expectation | Design expectation | Review depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Bachelor` | One bounded question, system, analysis, review, or design artifact | Honest application, replication, comparison, local evidence, implementation, or synthesis can be sufficient | Demonstrate correct fundamentals; avoid unjustified advanced methods | Supervisor rubric, reproducibility basics, citation and result integrity |
| `Master` | One coherent study or tightly linked set of studies | A defensible empirical, methodological, theoretical, or applied contribution | Robust controls, sample-size logic, uncertainty, reproducibility, and fallback | Thesis plus venue-style methodological and evidence audit |
| `Doctoral` | A coherent research program with linked work packages | Original, cumulative contribution with clear field position | Strong identification, independent validation, cross-study integration, amendments, and long-term provenance | Adversarial review, contribution program audit, publication and defense readiness |
| `Other` | Follow the institution's actual rubric | Do not infer degree-level novelty | Use the least complex valid design | Match the stated assessment |

Never tell a bachelor's student that a thesis requires field-changing novelty. Never weaken citation, data, statistics, ethics, or safety standards because the project is undergraduate.

## 2. Project tracks

### Literature

Use for narrative/scoping/systematic reviews, theory, legal/textual analysis, historical work, and source-based synthesis. Focus on a bounded question, search/source strategy, evidence matrix, synthesis logic, limitations, thesis, and defense. Do not create experiment or randomization files.

### Empirical

Use for surveys, interviews, observations, secondary data, field studies, behavioral work, and human-subject research. Define sampling, unit of analysis, instrument/measure validity, inclusion, missingness, confounding, analysis, privacy, consent, and ethics. Qualitative work needs coding, reflexivity, saturation/information-power reasoning, and an audit trail rather than inappropriate power calculations.

### WetLab

Use for biology, biochemistry, chemistry, medicine, agriculture, materials, environment, cells, animals, assays, and instruments. Load experimental design and integrity references. Require units, controls, independent versus technical replication, batches, QC, statistics, and approval status.

### Computational

Use for simulation, AI/ML, bioinformatics, numerical, and data-science work. Record dataset/version, splits, leakage checks, baseline, code revision, environment, seed/run count, metrics, compute budget, failures, and artifacts.

### Engineering

Use for software, hardware, embedded systems, information systems, prototypes, and system integration. Create a requirement-to-test matrix, architecture, implementation plan, versioned build, verification/validation, baseline, performance/security/reliability checks, and demo evidence. A working demo alone is not a research claim.

### Design

Use for industrial, visual, interaction, architectural, product, service, and creative capstones. Establish user/context evidence, design brief, criteria, iterations, decision rationale, evaluation plan, accessibility/ethics, final artifact, and reflective limitations. Do not force hypothesis testing when the assessment is design-led.

### General

Use only until the track is known. Create the core workspace and ask the smallest question needed to select a track before adding specialized artifacts.

## 3. Adaptive artifact policy

The 2.2 initializer uses these sets:

- Core, all profiles/tracks: 19 files, including canonical state, status, milestones, weekly/meeting/AI-use ledgers, research brief, decisions, topic, novelty, evidence, proposal, technical route, claim map, outline, manuscript, review matrix, defense outline, and questions.
- `Empirical`, `WetLab`, `Computational`: add six design/execution/analysis files.
- `Engineering`: add implementation plan, test matrix, and analysis log.
- `Design`: add design-process, evaluation-plan, and analysis log.
- `Master` and `Doctoral`: add rebuttal/revision response.
- `Doctoral`: add research-program and work-package files.

Expected initialized totals are: Bachelor Literature 19; Bachelor Engineering/Design 22; Master WetLab 26; Doctoral Computational 28. Counts are contract checks, not a reason to create every possible file.

Do not create unused files merely to make the project look rigorous. Add an artifact later when a stage or track actually needs it.

## 4. Anti-overengineering rules

- Prefer one answerable RQ and one backup for a bachelor's project.
- Prefer the simplest design that answers the question and matches available units, time, equipment, data, and skills.
- Do not recommend meta-analysis, structural equation modeling, deep learning, response surfaces, or complex mixed models merely because they sound advanced.
- Require a method only when it changes an inference or assessment outcome.
- Use the institution's rubric as the acceptance contract.
- Allocate time for data cleaning, failures, writing, formatting, supervisor feedback, and defense; do not spend the entire schedule on data collection or implementation.
- Treat a negative, null, replication, engineering, or design outcome as valid when the question, method, evidence, and limitations are handled correctly.

## 5. Undergraduate completion gates

### Topic and proposal

- The question is understandable, bounded, feasible in the remaining term, and aligned with the supervisor/institution.
- The contribution is stated honestly as application, comparison, replication, implementation, synthesis, design, or local evidence when appropriate.
- Required data, equipment, participants, materials, software, permissions, and supervision are realistically available.
- A minimal route and fallback can still produce an assessable thesis or capstone.

### Execution and analysis

- The student can explain the unit, comparison, measurement, method, and limitation.
- Files, versions, decisions, raw evidence, failures, and exclusions are preserved.
- The analysis matches the data and design; advanced statistics are not used decoratively.
- Safety, ethics, privacy, copyright, and academic-integrity requirements are met.

### Thesis and defense

- The thesis answers its own question rather than imitating a journal article's tone.
- Every central claim traces to literature, data, implementation/test evidence, or an explicit inference.
- The student can explain what was personally completed, what tools assisted, what failed, and what remains uncertain.
- Slides fit the time limit and expose limitations instead of hiding them.
